Bali Visa on Arrival 2026: Complete Guide for Indian Tourists

Published: August 1, 2026 7 min read By Ajit Mane Updated 2026

Bali is easily one of the most loved international destinations for Indian travellers. Between its iconic rice terraces at Tegallalang, the cliff-top temples of Uluwatu, lively beach clubs in Seminyak, and the spiritual calm of Ubud, the Island of the Gods offers something for every kind of traveller. Families, honeymooners, backpackers, and first-time international flyers all find their way there — and for Indian citizens, the good news is that getting in has never been easier.

Indian passport holders can enter Bali and the rest of Indonesia through the Visa on Arrival (VoA) facility, which was expanded to include India several years ago and remains available for tourists in 2026. This guide explains whether you need a visa, what the VoA costs, how to get it at Ngurah Rai Airport, how to extend it, and how it compares with the B211A visa for those planning a longer stay.

Key Fact

India is on Indonesia's visa-on-arrival list. Indian citizens can buy a VoA on arrival at Bali's Ngurah Rai airport for around IDR 500,000 (roughly USD 31), valid for 30 days and extendable once for another 30 days.

Do Indians Need a Visa for Bali?

Strictly speaking, yes — Indian citizens do need an entry visa to enter Indonesia, but they do not need to apply for one in advance. Indonesia offers an efficient Visa on Arrival scheme for visitors from eligible countries, including India. You simply purchase the visa at the immigration counter when you land.

The VoA is designed for short tourist stays and is available at airports, major seaports, and land border checkpoints across Indonesia, including Ngurah Rai International Airport in Bali, which is where the overwhelming majority of Indian tourists arrive. If you fly into Jakarta, Yogyakarta, or any other international airport, the same facility applies.

There is no requirement to print anything before departure, no embassy appointment, and no lengthy application form. As long as you have a valid passport, a return or onward ticket, and proof of accommodation, you can complete the process in a few minutes after disembarking.

Bali Visa on Arrival: Costs and Validity

The cost and validity of the Bali Visa on Arrival have remained stable, and this is what travellers should budget for in 2026:

Item Details
VoA fee IDR 500,000 for 30 days (approximately USD 31 / INR 2,600)
Initial validity 30 days from the date of entry
Extension One-time extension of 30 days, renewable once (fee approximately IDR 500,000)
Maximum stay Up to 60 days (30 + 30) with extension
Payment methods Cash (IDR, USD, or other major currencies) and major credit cards at most airports
Who it's for Tourism, short business meetings, and social visits only
Pro Tip: Keep the VoA receipt and passport stickers safe. You will need them for your extension application and when you exit Indonesia, and losing them can mean extra paperwork at departure.

How to Get Visa on Arrival at Bali Airport

The process at Ngurah Rai Airport (DPS) is quick and well signposted. Here is the exact step-by-step sequence:

1

Fill Out the Digital Customs and Immigration Form

Before you land, complete the Indonesian eCD (Customs Declaration) and any required health declarations online. You can use the official 'Customs Indonesia' website on your phone; one form covers the whole family.

2

Walk to the Visa on Arrival Counters

After disembarking, follow the signs to 'Visa on Arrival' before the immigration queues. There are dedicated payment counters where you pay the IDR 500,000 fee.

3

Pay the Fee and Get Your Sticker

Pay in cash or by card. Make sure the officer issues the VoA sticker and stamps it into your passport. Some travellers also complete the payment via the online 'visa on arrival' e-payment options available at select airports.

4

Go Through Immigration Control

Present your passport, VoA sticker, and boarding documents to the immigration officer. If asked, show your return ticket and hotel booking. The officer will stamp you in for 30 days.

5

Collect Luggage and Exit

After clearing immigration, collect your bags, scan the customs QR code you prepared, and exit into the arrivals hall where your transport should be waiting.

Extending Your Bali Visa

If 30 days is not enough, the VoA can be extended once for another 30 days, giving you a maximum stay of 60 days. The extension is processed by the Indonesian immigration office (Kantor Imigrasi) and requires a short in-person visit.

Important: Apply for the extension early — ideally at least 7–14 days before your stamp expires. Extensions are not granted automatically, and leaving it to the last day risks an overstay, which carries a fine of IDR 1,000,000 per day.

The common route used by many Indian tourists is door-to-door agent-assisted extension, where a licensed visa agent submits your documents and handles the waiting. The alternative is to visit the Ngurah Rai or Denpasar immigration office yourself. Either way, you must appear in person for biometric checks at some stage. Fees for the on-site extension are typically around IDR 500,000, plus agent service fees if you use one.

Always keep your passport with the VoA stamp valid for at least two weeks beyond your planned exit when applying, since Indonesian immigration generally requires the passport to remain valid for 30 days from the extension date.

Bali Visa on Arrival vs B211A Visa

For stays longer than 60 days, or for travel that is not purely touristic, the B211A visa (also called the Social Cultural visa or 'budget tourist visa') is the alternative. Here is how the two compare:

Feature Visa on Arrival (VoA) B211A Visa
Application On arrival, at the airport Online, in advance with an agent or sponsor
Cost IDR 500,000 About IDR 1,500,000 + agency fees
Initial validity 30 days 60 days
Maximum stay 60 days (with one extension) Up to 180 days (4 extensions)
Best suited for Short holidays of up to 2 months Long stays, digital nomad-style trips, visiting friends

Most Indian tourists visiting for a honeymoon or a two-to-three-week holiday will find the VoA more than sufficient and far cheaper. Choose B211A only if you know from the start that you will stay beyond 60 days.

Essential Tips for Indian Travellers

Make your Bali trip smoother with these practical pointers:

  • Carry USD or IDR cash for the VoA fee; airport exchange counters are available but rates are better in town.
  • Book accommodation before you fly — Indonesian immigration commonly asks for proof of lodging and a return ticket.
  • Get an eSIM or local SIM card after arrival; Grab, Gojek, and ride-hailing apps need a working number.
  • Respect temple etiquette: wear a sarong at sacred sites and dress modestly, especially around Kuta to Uluwatu temples.
  • Drink bottled water and be careful with spicy local food if you have a sensitive stomach; Bali has excellent medical care but it is expensive without insurance.
  • Do not work on a VoA. Remote working is technically not allowed under this visa category, so keep it strictly as a tourist visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do Indian citizens need a visa to visit Bali in 2026?
A: Yes, but you can get it on arrival. India is on Indonesia's visa-on-arrival list, so Indian citizens can purchase a VoA at Ngurah Rai Airport for IDR 500,000, valid for 30 days and extendable once.

Q: How much does the Bali visa on arrival cost for Indians?
A: The official VoA fee is IDR 500,000 per person. This converts to roughly USD 31 or about INR 2,600, depending on the exchange rate. You can pay in cash or by card at most airports.

Q: Can I extend my Bali visa on arrival?
A: Yes, the VoA can be extended once for another 30 days, bringing your total stay to 60 days. Apply at an Indonesian immigration office (or through a licensed agent) well before your current stamp expires.

Q: What documents do I need for visa on arrival in Bali?
A: You need a valid Indian passport with at least 6 months validity and 2 blank pages, a return or onward flight ticket within 30 days, proof of accommodation, sufficient funds, and the visa fee of IDR 500,000.

Q: What happens if I overstay my Bali visa on arrival?
A: Overstaying invites a fine of IDR 1,000,000 per day, and serious overstays can lead to detention, deportation, and a re-entry ban. Always extend on time or exit Indonesia before your stamped expiry date.
