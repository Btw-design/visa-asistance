Malaysia Visa-Free for Indians 2026: What You Need to Know Before You Go

Published: August 1, 2026 7 min read By Ajit Mane Updated 2026

Malaysia has long been one of the most popular destinations for Indian travellers, offering a rare mix of futuristic city skylines, tropical islands, rainforests, and a rich cultural heritage that feels instantly familiar. For years, Indian passport holders had to obtain a visa before flying, usually through the eNTRI scheme, an eVisa, or a traditional paper visa routed through the Malaysian mission. In December 2023, that changed when Malaysia announced visa-free entry for Indian citizens for stays of up to 30 days. The policy has been extended in stages and remains in effect for travellers arriving in 2026.

If you are planning a holiday to Kuala Lumpur, Penang, Langkawi, or Borneo, this guide breaks down everything you need to know about the Malaysia visa-free policy for Indians in 2026 — who qualifies, what documents you need, how entry works at the airport, and practical tips for a smooth trip.

Key Fact

The visa-free arrangement grants Indian citizens entry for tourism, business visits, and social purposes for up to 30 days. There is no visa fee, no eNTRI fee, and no pre-approval step — you simply present your passport and travel documents at immigration on arrival.

Malaysia Visa-Free Entry: What Changed?

The visa exemption for Indian nationals was first introduced in December 2023 alongside similar waivers for China and other countries, as part of Malaysia's push to revive its tourism sector. Instead of the previous eNTRI scheme — which charged a processing fee and restricted travellers to specific ports of entry — the new arrangement is a genuine visa waiver. You no longer need to register online, pay a fee, or obtain approval before departure for standard tourist travel.

Under the policy as it stands for 2026, Indian citizens can enter Malaysia without a visa and stay for up to 30 days. The exemption covers tourism, attending short business meetings, and social visits. It applies at air, sea, and land entry points. The Malaysian government has reviewed and renewed the scheme in stages since its launch, and visitors should keep an eye on official announcements for any changes to the end date.

Important: The visa-free stay of 30 days is not extendable. If you wish to remain in Malaysia longer, you cannot simply show up and extend the waiver. Overstaying attracts daily fines and can lead to being blacklisted from re-entering Malaysia.

Eligibility Requirements

Most Indian citizens qualify for visa-free entry, but you must meet a few basic conditions to avoid being refused at the border. Here is who is eligible:

  • You hold a valid Indian passport with at least six months of remaining validity from your date of entry.
  • Your travel purpose is tourism, business visits, or social visits.
  • You hold a confirmed return or onward ticket proving you will leave Malaysia within 30 days.
  • You have sufficient funds to cover your stay and are not intending to work or seek employment.
  • You have no history of visa violations, overstays, or blacklisting in Malaysia.
Pro Tip: Children travelling with their parents are covered by the same visa-free facility, but each child must hold an individual valid passport. Do not rely on a parent's passport at Malaysian immigration.

Documents Needed for Entry

The visa-free facility keeps document requirements minimal, but immigration officers still expect to see evidence that you are a genuine visitor. Carry the following in hand luggage, ideally as printed copies:

Entry Documents Checklist:
  • Valid Indian passport with at least six months validity and two blank pages.
  • Confirmed return or onward air ticket dated within 30 days of arrival.
  • Hotel bookings or a letter from your host confirming where you are staying.
  • Proof of sufficient funds — cash, cards, or a recent bank statement.
  • Travel insurance covering medical emergencies (strongly recommended).
  • Copy of your visa-free policy reference or this guide (optional but useful at verification counters).

How to Enter Malaysia Visa-Free

Entering Malaysia under the visa-free policy is straightforward. Follow these steps to keep the process quick and stress-free:

1

Confirm Your Dates and Eligibility

Check that your stay does not exceed 30 days and that your passport has sufficient validity. If your trip is longer than 30 days, explore alternatives such as exiting and re-entering or applying for a proper visa in advance.

2

Book Flights and Accommodation

Reserve your return flight and first night's accommodation. Malaysian immigration frequently asks to see a confirmed onward ticket and hotel confirmation, so have these documents ready.

3

Prepare Your Documents

Pack your passport, return ticket, hotel bookings, and proof of funds in your carry-on. Keep a printed copy of each in case your phone battery dies or the officer requests physical copies.

4

Arrive and Head to Immigration

Upon arrival at KLIA, Penang, Langkawi, or any other entry point, proceed directly to the immigration counter for foreign tourists. There is no visa application desk, fee payment, or approval process needed for visa-free entry.

5

Get Stamped for 30 Days

Present your passport and documents. Most officers will stamp you in for up to 30 days. Verify the stamp and the number of days granted before leaving the counter.

Malaysia Visa-Free vs Visa on Arrival

Indians sometimes hear about Malaysia's Visa on Arrival (VoA) facility and wonder whether it is relevant. For standard tourist visits it is not needed, but understanding the difference helps you avoid confusion at the airport.

Feature Visa-Free Entry (for Indians) Visa on Arrival (VoA)
Fee Free RM 200 (paid at the counter)
Max stay Up to 30 days Up to 15 days
Application None — automatic for eligible travellers Counter application at limited entry points
Entry restriction All air, sea, and land entry points Confined to select points, including arrival via Thailand, Singapore, or Brunei
Extension Not extendable Not extendable

In short, visa-free entry is faster, cheaper, and simpler for Indian tourists. You should not pay for a VoA at the airport if you qualify for the waiver — doing so only adds cost and paperwork with no benefit.

Travel Tips for Indian Visitors

A few practical habits can make your Malaysian trip smoother and more enjoyable:

  • Carry some Malaysian Ringgit in cash for taxis, local markets, and small vendors, though cards are widely accepted in cities.
  • Buy a local SIM or eSIM on arrival; data plans are inexpensive and connectivity in Malaysia is excellent.
  • The best travel windows are generally December to February and June to August, when the weather is most favourable for most of the country.
  • Respect local customs — dress modestly at religious sites and remove shoes before entering mosques and temples.
  • Stay within your 30-day window. If plans change, check your onward ticket immediately rather than risking an overstay.
  • Keep digital copies of your passport and entry stamp in cloud storage in case of loss or theft.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do Indians need a visa for Malaysia in 2026?
A: No. Indian citizens can enter Malaysia without a visa for stays of up to 30 days under the visa-free policy that remains in effect in 2026. The entry covers tourism, business visits, and social visits.

Q: How long can an Indian stay in Malaysia without a visa?
A: Up to 30 days per entry. The period is granted at the immigration counter on arrival and cannot be extended under the visa-free facility.

Q: Do I need to pay any fee for visa-free entry into Malaysia?
A: No. Visa-free entry for eligible Indian travellers is completely free. You do not pay a fee at the airport or register online before departure.

Q: What happens if I overstay my 30 days in Malaysia?
A: Overstaying is taken seriously in Malaysia. You will be fined for each day of overstay, and in serious cases you may be detained, deported, and blacklisted from re-entering the country. Always leave before your stamped date expires.

Q: Can the Malaysia visa-free period be extended for a longer stay?
A: No, the 30-day visa-free period cannot be extended. For stays longer than 30 days, you may need to exit and re-enter, or apply for an appropriate visa such as a Malaysian eVisa or a long-term social visit pass through official channels.
