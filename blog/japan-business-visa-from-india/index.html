Visa facts checked against the Embassy of Japan in India and the VFS checklists on 13 October 2026

This guide is for Indian professionals heading to Japan on company business: employees sent by their company, founders and business owners meeting partners or suppliers, and teams attending conferences or trade fairs. Each needs a slightly different file, and those differences are what usually cause delays.

What a Japan business visa actually is

Indian passport holders need a visa before any business trip to Japan; there is no visa-free entry or visa on arrival. What people call a “business visa” is officially a Short-Term Stay (Temporary Visitor) visa for business purposes: up to 90 days per visit, with no pay or income from a Japanese source.

If a Japanese company will employ or pay you, you need a work visa backed by a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) instead. That is outside this guide.

Who can apply and what you can do

Indian nationals living in India, and foreign nationals legally resident in India, can apply. You need an invitation from a company or organisation in Japan.

Allowed activities on a short-term business visa:

  • Business meetings, liaison and negotiations
  • Signing contracts
  • After-sales service visits
  • Market research and advertising work
  • Attending conferences, seminars and trade fairs
  • Cultural, sports or academic exchanges

Not allowed: any paid work in Japan, running a revenue-generating operation there, or receiving remuneration. The permitted stay cannot be extended, so plan the trip around the days granted at landing.

Which documents depend on your role

The core file is the same for everyone (next sections). What changes is who vouches for you in India and how you prove your finances.

For trade fairs and conferences, the invitation can come from the organiser, an exhibitor or the Japanese partner you will meet. Attach your event registration or exhibitor confirmation so the dates and purpose are easy to verify.

eVISA or sticker visa? Single or multiple entry?

Business travellers get a traditional sticker visa through VFS Global, not the eVISA. Japan’s eVISA in India, live since 1 April 2024, covers only single-entry short-term tourism visas. See our guide to the Japan tourist eVISA.

OptionWho it suitsValidityStay per visit
Single-entry businessFirst-time or occasional visitors3 monthsUp to 90 days
Multiple-entry businessFrequent visitors who meet the criteria below1, 3, 5 or 10 years15, 30 or 90 days

You may qualify for a multiple-entry business visa if you are a regular employee of:

  • A government or public enterprise
  • A company listed on a stock exchange (India, Japan or elsewhere), or its subsidiary, joint venture or branch
  • A Japanese-affiliated company that is a member of the Japan Chamber of Commerce
  • A private company with constant business dealings with a listed Japanese company

Frequent travellers also qualify: more than three business trips to Japan in the last three years, or one business trip to Japan plus visits to other G7 countries in that period. Your spouse and children can be included. The embassy may still issue a single-entry visa after review.

Documents checklist

The file has two halves: what you prepare in India, and what your host prepares in Japan. All photocopies must be on A4 paper. This list follows the VFS short-term business checklist.

Prepared by you (the applicant)

  • Passport with at least two blank pages, plus any old passports
  • Photocopy of the passport’s bio-data (first and last) pages
  • Visa application form, fully completed and signed
  • One passport-size photo, white background, taken within 6 months
  • Travel order or covering letter from your employer
  • Certificate of employment (business owners: company registration and proof of ownership instead)
  • Flight booking to and from Japan
  • Latest income tax return (needed if you want multiple entry)
  • Proof of relationship, if a dependent travels with you

Prepared by your host in Japan

  • Invitation letter (states the purpose and dates of the visit)
  • Itinerary in Japan, with hotel and contact details
  • List of applicants, if several people travel together

Prepared by whoever pays for the trip (if it is the Japanese side)

  • Letter of guarantee
  • Certified copy of the company’s incorporation register, or a company overview

Documents from Japan should be issued within the last three months; copies are usually accepted. The embassy can ask for more documents or an interview.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Get the invitation package from Japan. Ask your host company for the invitation letter, itinerary and, if they are paying, the letter of guarantee and company documents.
  2. Collect your own documents. Work through the checklist above, including the employer’s travel order and employment certificate.
  3. Fill in the visa application form. Download it from the VFS Japan India site, complete it, sign it and attach your photo.
  4. Find your VFS centre. Apply at the VFS Japan centre covering the state where you live; each centre falls under the Embassy in New Delhi or a Consulate-General (Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai or Bengaluru).
  5. Submit in person. No prior appointment is needed. Applications are accepted up to 3 months before your travel date.
  6. Pay the fees. Pay the visa fee plus the VFS service charge at the centre.
  7. Track and collect. Track the application on the VFS site and collect your passport with the visa sticker once it is ready.

Fees and processing time

The visa fee for Indian nationals is ₹500, whether single or multiple entry, under fees effective 1 July 2026 (Embassy of Japan in India). The separate transit visa has been abolished.

Processing usually takes about 5 to 10 working days, but the embassy warns that times vary with application volume and cannot be expedited. Apply early, ideally 4 to 6 weeks before you fly.

Tips to avoid rejection

  • Make the invitation name the business. “Meetings with ABC Corp, Osaka, on supplying auto components, 12 to 16 November” beats “business visit”. Vague purposes are a common reason for queries.
  • Match every date. Invitation, itinerary, flight booking, covering letter and application form should show the same travel dates.
  • Founders: show the business is real. Incorporation papers, three years of company ITRs and a company bank statement carry more weight than a personal letter.
  • Trade-fair visitors: attach the registration. An exhibitor badge confirmation or delegate pass ties your dates to a verifiable event.
  • Use fresh Japanese documents. Anything from your host should be under three months old.
  • Plan for multiple entry early. If you will visit Japan often, ask your employer whether it qualifies (listed company, PSU, Japanese-affiliated firm) and apply for multiple entry from the start.
  • Never mention paid work. Installation or consulting paid by the Japanese side needs a work visa, not this one.
  • Beware of fraud. The embassy never asks for payment by phone or email to speed up a visa; pay only the visa fee and VFS charge.

Final word

A Japan business visa is cheap and quick for Indian travellers, provided the paperwork from both sides lines up. Start with your Japanese host’s invitation, build your file from the checklist, and submit at VFS well ahead of travel. Rules changed more than once in 2026, so check the embassy page before you apply.
