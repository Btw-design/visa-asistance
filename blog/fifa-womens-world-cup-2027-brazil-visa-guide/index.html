Tournament and visa facts checked against FIFA and the Embassy of Brazil in New Delhi on 6 October 2026

The next FIFA World Cup is in 2027: the FIFA Women’s World Cup, hosted by Brazil from 24 June to 25 July 2027. It is the first time the tournament comes to South America, with 32 teams playing 64 matches in eight cities.

If you plan to travel from India to watch, you need a Brazilian visa before you fly. This guide covers the tournament dates and host cities, and what the Embassy of Brazil in New Delhi asks for: the visa type, fee, documents and application steps.

Do Indians need a visa for Brazil?

Yes. The Embassy of Brazil in New Delhi states that Indian citizens are required to obtain a visa before travelling to Brazil. There is no visa on arrival.

Fans apply for a Visitor Visa (VIVIS). The Embassy lists tourism and “cultural, educational, sports or recreational activities” among the purposes it covers. You may not do paid work in Brazil on this visa.

Spectators need proof of the event. The Embassy says that if you are attending an event as a spectator, you must submit proof of registration or a copy of the confirmation that you have signed up to attend. For a World Cup trip, keep your ticket confirmation ready.

Visa fee and processing time

ItemWhat the Embassy says
Visitor Visa (VIVIS) feeINR 9,600 (regular fee)
How to payBank transfer from the applicant’s own bank account; cash deposits are not accepted
RefundsThe fee is non-refundable
Processing timeCan take up to 10–15 business days due to high demand

The applicant’s name must show on the Embassy’s bank statement, so do not pay from an agent’s or another person’s account. Payment details appear at the last step of the appointment form.

Documents required

The Embassy lists these documents for a Visitor Visa:

  • Online visa application form (signed by both parents for minors)
  • Passport, original and copy: signed, valid for at least 6 months, with at least 2 blank pages
  • Proof of financial capability, such as bank statements for the past three months, an income tax return or a pay slip
  • Passport-style photo that meets ICAO requirements, taken in the last 6 months
  • Flight tickets: a booked itinerary showing entry into and exit from Brazil
  • Personal cover letter with details of the applicant and the trip
  • Hotel reservation or similar document
  • Proof of registration or confirmation for the event you are attending as a spectator
  • For minors: a visa authorisation form signed by both parents and notarised

The Embassy also publishes a document checklist for the tourist visa. For photo rules, see our visa photo requirements tool.

How to apply from India

  1. Fill in the online visa application form and print the receipt. Completing the form does not submit your application.
  2. Request an appointment through the Embassy’s e-consular system and upload what it asks for. Do not send documents by email.
  3. Pay the visa fee by bank transfer at the last step of the appointment form.
  4. Wait for the Embassy to validate your request, which it says takes 24 to 48 hours. You then receive an email link to book your appointment.
  5. Go to the Embassy at your appointment time with your passport and all original supporting documents. Walk-in submissions are not accepted.

The Embassy’s submission counter works Monday to Thursday, 10:00 am to 12:00 pm. It does not accept applications sent by courier, but an authorised third party can hand in your application.

Living in Maharashtra, Goa or Karnataka? You may also apply at the Consulate General of Brazil in Mumbai.

How long can you stay?

Activities on a Visitor Visa must not exceed 90 days. The tournament runs for 32 days, so one visit covers the whole event with time to travel.

Extensions are handled only inside Brazil, by the Brazilian Federal Police: up to a further 90 days, to a maximum of 180 days in any twelve-month period.

Planning tips for Indian fans

  • Apply early. The Embassy warns that high demand affects both processing times and the availability of submission appointments, and advises applicants to plan well in advance.
  • Buy tickets only from FIFA. Ticket sales and prices are announced on FIFA’s official website. You will need your ticket confirmation for the visa application.
  • Check the yellow fever rule. The Embassy says a yellow fever certificate is not required to enter Brazil, but Indian health officials require proof of vaccination when you return to India.
  • Choose the right category. The Embassy does not refund fees paid for the wrong visa category. If you are travelling to work at the event rather than watch it, ask the Embassy which visa applies.
  • Plan your route between cities. Host cities are spread across Brazil, from Porto Alegre in the south to Fortaleza in the north.
