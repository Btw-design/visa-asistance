K-ETA for Indians 2026: Complete Guide to South Korea's Electronic Travel Auth

Published: August 1, 2026 9 min read By Ajit Mane Updated 2026

South Korea has fast become one of the most popular travel destinations for Indian tourists, fuelled by the global wave of K-pop and K-dramas, world-class shopping, and a rich blend of ancient culture and cutting-edge technology. In 2026, Indian travellers benefit from one of the easiest routes into the country through the Korea Electronic Travel Authorization, or K-ETA, which lets you enter South Korea for short visits without a traditional paper visa.

If you are an Indian passport holder planning to visit Seoul, Busan, or Jeju Island, understanding exactly how K-ETA works can save you from last-minute stress and entry refusals at immigration. This complete guide explains what K-ETA is, who is eligible, how to apply step by step, what it costs, and the key mistakes Indian applicants should avoid in 2026.

Key Fact

Indian citizens travelling to South Korea for tourism, visiting friends or family, or short business trips must obtain an approved K-ETA before they board their flight. The authorization is valid for multiple entries over up to three years, and each visit can last up to 90 days.

What is K-ETA?

K-ETA (Korea Electronic Travel Authorization) is a mandatory pre-screening system operated by the South Korean government. It is designed for passport holders of visa-exempt countries — including India — who wish to enter South Korea for short stays. Instead of applying for a paper visa at an embassy, eligible travellers submit a simple online application, pay a small fee, and receive approval electronically within a matter of hours.

K-ETA is not a visa. It is an electronically-linked travel authorization, similar in concept to the US ESTA or Canada's eTA. Once approved, it is connected to your passport number, so there is no stamping, visa label, or embassy visit involved. At immigration, the officer scans your passport, verifies the authorization, and, in most cases, you are through in under a minute.

Here are the key details every Indian applicant should know:

  • Validity: Up to three years from the date of approval, covering multiple entries.
  • Maximum stay: Up to 90 days per visit, subject to the immigration officer's discretion.
  • Application mode: 100% online — no embassy appointment required.
  • Fee: Approximately USD 10 (around KRW 13,300) per person, non-refundable.
  • Processing time: Typically 24 to 72 hours; most applications are cleared within a day.
  • Applies to: Indian citizens holding a normal (blue) passport travelling for short visits.

K-ETA Eligibility for Indian Citizens

India is on South Korea's K-ETA eligible country list, which means most Indian citizens can apply for K-ETA instead of a traditional visa. However, eligibility depends on your travel purpose and personal profile. Here is who can apply:

  • You must hold a valid Indian passport with at least 6 months of validity remaining.
  • Your trip must be for tourism, visiting friends or family, or short-term business such as meetings and conferences.
  • You must have no criminal record, deportation history, or previous visa violations in South Korea or other countries.
  • You must not be returning to South Korea after overstaying a previous K-ETA or visa period.
  • Your passport must not be reported lost or stolen, and you must travel using the same passport you applied with.
  • Children and senior citizens can apply individually — each traveller needs their own application, and minors under 14 cannot authorise their own approval.
Important: If your travel purpose is study, long-term employment, or permanent residence, K-ETA does not apply. You will need to apply for a proper visa category through the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in India instead.

How to Apply for K-ETA

Applying for K-ETA is a straightforward online process that takes about 10 to 15 minutes. Follow these five steps carefully:

1

Visit the Official K-ETA Website

Go to the official Korea Electronic Travel Authorization portal and select your preferred language. Beware of third-party websites that charge inflated fees — always use the official government portal for your application.

2

Fill in the Application Form

Enter your personal details, passport information, and travel plans carefully. Make sure your name and passport number exactly match your passport — even a single typo can cause delays or rejection.

3

Upload Photo and Passport

Upload a recent passport-style photograph and a clear copy of your passport bio page. The photo must meet the official specifications — white background, no glasses, and taken within the last six months.

4

Pay the Application Fee

Pay the non-refundable fee of around USD 10 using an international credit or debit card. Keep the payment receipt and your application reference number handy for tracking.

5

Submit and Track Your Application

Submit the application and wait for the result. You will receive an email confirmation, and you can check the status on the official portal using your application number. Once approved, download the PDF confirmation and keep a soft or hard copy with your travel documents.

Pro Tip: Apply for your K-ETA at least two weeks before your departure date. While most applications are approved within 24 hours, borderline cases can take longer, and airlines may refuse boarding without a valid approval.

K-ETA vs Visa on Arrival

A common question from Indian travellers is whether South Korea offers a visa on arrival. Unlike some other Asian destinations, South Korea does not provide a general visa-on-arrival facility for Indian tourists. Visa-exempt travellers must hold a pre-approved K-ETA; everyone else must apply for a visa before travel. Here is how the options compare:

Important Tips for Indian Applicants

To ensure a smooth K-ETA experience in 2026, keep this checklist handy before you apply or travel:

K-ETA Application Checklist:
  • Apply at least 2–3 weeks before your flight to allow time for processing.
  • Double-check that your name, passport number, and date of birth match your passport exactly.
  • Use a photo with a white background, no glasses, and no filters or editing.
  • Apply only through the official K-ETA portal — avoid agents charging hundreds of dollars.
  • Keep a printed or offline copy of your approval PDF with your travel documents.
  • Carry return flight tickets and proof of accommodation to show at immigration if asked.
  • Verify your passport has at least 6 months validity before your travel date.
  • If your application is rejected, do not fly — instead apply for a regular C-3-9 visa.
Pro Tip: One approved K-ETA is enough for multiple visits within its validity period. If you plan to visit South Korea more than once, you do not need to reapply until it expires.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is K-ETA mandatory for Indian citizens travelling to South Korea?
A: Yes. Indian citizens travelling to South Korea for tourism, family visits, or short business trips are required to obtain an approved K-ETA before departure. Without a valid approval, airlines may refuse boarding and entry can be denied at immigration.

Q: How many days before travel should I apply for K-ETA?
A: It is best to apply at least 2–3 weeks before your departure. Although most applications are approved within 24–72 hours, applying early gives you time to correct errors or, if needed, switch to a regular visa application.

Q: What happens if my K-ETA application is rejected?
A: If your K-ETA is rejected, you cannot travel visa-free. Your best option is to apply for a regular South Korea tourist visa (C-3-9) through the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in India. The application fee is non-refundable, and you can usually reapply with corrected information.

Q: Can I extend my stay in South Korea beyond 90 days?
A: No. K-ETA permits a maximum stay of up to 90 days per visit. If you need to stay longer, you must leave the country or apply for a different visa category through the immigration office before your authorised stay expires.

Q: Does an approved K-ETA guarantee entry into South Korea?
A: No. K-ETA approval allows you to board a flight and present yourself at immigration, but the final decision to grant entry rests with the immigration officer, who may ask for additional documents such as return tickets, hotel bookings, or proof of funds.
