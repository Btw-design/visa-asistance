Planning a European holiday often starts with one important question: Schengen visa for which countries is valid?

As of 2026, the Schengen Area consists of 29 European countries. A valid Schengen visa generally allows eligible travellers to visit these countries during the same trip without applying for a separate short-stay visa for every destination.

For Indian travellers planning to explore destinations such as France, Switzerland, Italy, Spain, Germany, Greece or the Netherlands, understanding which countries come under the Schengen visa can make planning a multi-country European trip much easier.

What is a Schengen visa?

A Schengen visa is a short-stay visa that allows eligible non-EU travellers to visit countries within the Schengen Area.

A standard short-stay Schengen visa permits a stay of up to 90 days within any 180-day period, subject to the validity and conditions stated on the visa.

Depending on the visa issued, it may allow:

  • Single entry into the Schengen Area
  • Multiple entries during the visa validity period
  • Travel between participating Schengen countries
  • Tourism
  • Family or friend visits
  • Business trips and other permitted short-stay purposes

A short-stay Schengen visa does not automatically permit employment or stays exceeding 90 days. Longer stays are generally governed by the national immigration rules of the destination country.

There are currently 29 countries in the Schengen Area: 25 EU countries and four non-EU countries.

Although Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland are not EU member states, they participate in the Schengen Area and apply the common short-stay visa framework.

Are Bulgaria and Romania Schengen countries?

Yes. Bulgaria and Romania became full members of the Schengen Area on 1 January 2025.

Air and sea internal border controls were initially lifted on 31 March 2024, followed by the removal of internal land border controls from 1 January 2025. Therefore, both Bulgaria and Romania are included in the 29-country Schengen list in 2026.

Travellers relying on older lists showing 27 Schengen countries should therefore check updated information before planning their journey.

Is Switzerland included in a Schengen visa?

Yes. Switzerland is a Schengen country, even though it is not a member of the European Union.

A valid Schengen visa can generally be used for travel to Switzerland subject to the conditions stated on the visa and applicable entry requirements.

The same applies to the other three non-EU Schengen members:

  • Iceland
  • Liechtenstein
  • Norway
  • Switzerland

Is the UK included in a Schengen visa?

No. The United Kingdom is not part of the Schengen Area. A Schengen visa by itself does not give a traveller permission to enter the UK.

Indian travellers planning a combined Europe and UK trip should check the UK's separate visa requirements. See our UK visa guide.

Is Ireland a Schengen country?

No. Although Ireland is a member of the European Union, it is not part of the Schengen Area and does not issue Schengen visas. Ireland applies its own national visa and immigration rules.

Is Cyprus a Schengen country?

Cyprus is an EU member state but is not currently a full member of the border-free Schengen Area because internal border controls have not yet been abolished.

Cyprus therefore does not issue Schengen visas under the same system as the 29 full Schengen states. Travellers should check Cyprus-specific entry requirements before travelling.

Is the European Union the same as the Schengen Area?

No. The European Union and the Schengen Area are not the same thing.

There are EU countries that are not full Schengen members, while several Schengen countries are not EU members.

EU but not full Schengen members

  • Ireland
  • Cyprus

Schengen members but not EU members

  • Iceland
  • Liechtenstein
  • Norway
  • Switzerland

This distinction is important when planning a European itinerary because simply being an EU country does not automatically mean that a Schengen visa applies in the same way. For more detail, read Schengen vs EU vs UK.

Do Indian citizens need a Schengen visa?

Yes. Indian citizens travelling to the Schengen Area for a short stay generally need to obtain a Schengen visa before travelling.

The European Commission specifically confirms that an Indian citizen travelling to the Schengen Area for a short stay requires a visa. Indian travellers who require a Schengen visa do not need ETIAS.

Depending on your travel history, Indian nationals may also qualify for longer-validity multiple-entry visas under the EU's visa “cascade” regime.

Under rules introduced for Indian nationals, eligible applicants with an established travel history may receive a two-year multiple-entry visa after having lawfully obtained and used two Schengen visas within the previous three years. A five-year visa may subsequently be possible if the required conditions, including passport validity, are met.

Which country should you apply to for a Schengen visa?

One of the most common misconceptions is that travellers can simply apply through whichever Schengen country offers the easiest or fastest appointment.

The correct country depends on your actual travel itinerary.

If you are visiting only one Schengen country

Apply through the consulate or authorised visa application channel for that country.

Example: if your entire trip is to France, you should apply for a French Schengen visa.

If you are visiting multiple Schengen countries

Apply through the country that is your main destination, generally the country where you will spend the longest period or where the primary purpose of your trip takes place.

Example:

  • France – 3 days
  • Switzerland – 4 days
  • Italy – 7 days

Italy is the longest stay, so Italy would normally be the appropriate country through which to submit the application. More on this in our guide to the main destination rule.

If your stay is equal in multiple countries

If there is no other main destination and you intend to spend an equal number of days in several Schengen countries, the country of first entry becomes relevant. See the first entry rule.

Official EU guidance requires applicants to apply through their actual main destination rather than selecting a country simply for visa convenience.

Can I enter through a different country after getting a Schengen visa?

In general, a valid uniform Schengen visa can permit entry through another Schengen country's external border.

However, your visa application should accurately represent your real travel plans, particularly your main destination. A visa also does not create an automatic right of entry; border authorities can verify whether travellers continue to meet entry requirements.

Travellers should therefore carry relevant documents such as:

  • Passport
  • Valid visa
  • Accommodation details
  • Return or onward travel details
  • Travel insurance
  • Evidence of sufficient funds
  • Documents supporting the purpose of travel

How long can you stay with a Schengen visa?

For a standard short-stay visa, the maximum permitted stay is generally 90 days in any rolling 180-day period.

This limit applies to your combined stay across the Schengen Area, not separately to each country.

For example, spending:

  • 20 days in France
  • 20 days in Switzerland
  • 20 days in Germany
  • 30 days in Italy

would equal 90 days in the Schengen Area.

You do not receive a separate 90-day allowance for each country. Always check the exact validity dates, number of permitted entries and authorised duration of stay shown on your visa sticker.

Schengen visa documents

Exact requirements can vary depending on the destination and purpose of travel, but applicants commonly need:

  • Valid passport
  • Completed visa application form
  • Recent photograph meeting applicable standards
  • Travel itinerary
  • Flight reservation or travel details
  • Proof of accommodation
  • Travel medical insurance
  • Proof of sufficient financial means
  • Employment, business or educational documents where applicable
  • Documents demonstrating the purpose of the visit
  • Supporting evidence of the applicant's circumstances and intended return

According to the European Commission, the passport should normally remain valid for at least three months after the intended departure from the Schengen Area.

Always follow the latest checklist issued by the relevant embassy, consulate or authorised visa application centre. Our Schengen visa guide covers fees and the application process.

2026 Schengen travel update: Entry/Exit System

Another important change for European travel is the Entry/Exit System (EES).

The EES became fully operational across participating European countries on 10 April 2026. It electronically records information relating to non-EU nationals crossing external borders for short stays, including travel document details, entry and exit information and biometric data.

Indian travellers holding Schengen visas may therefore experience updated digital border procedures when entering or leaving the Schengen Area.

ETIAS, by contrast, is intended for eligible visa-exempt travellers. Indian nationals who require a Schengen visa do not need ETIAS for that journey. As of the latest official EU update, ETIAS is not yet operational.

Final thoughts

A Schengen visa makes multi-country European travel significantly more convenient because 29 European countries participate in the Schengen Area in 2026.

The current Schengen countries are:

Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland.

Before submitting your application, make sure you identify the correct country based on your itinerary, understand the 90-days-in-180-days rule and prepare documents according to the latest requirements of the relevant diplomatic mission.

For Indian travellers planning a Europe trip, getting the destination, itinerary and visa documentation right from the beginning can help make the application process clearer and better organised.
