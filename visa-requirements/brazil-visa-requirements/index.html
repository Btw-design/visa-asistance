Brazil visa requirements for Indians: overview

Indian passport holders must get a visa before travelling to Brazil. Tourists apply in person for a Visitor Visa (VIVIS) at the Embassy in New Delhi or the Consulate General in Mumbai. There is no tourist e-visa: the online e-visa is only for business trips.

When you do not need a visa

You can enter without a visa if you hold one of the following:

  • A diplomatic or official Indian passport, for stays of up to 90 days

The Embassy also notes that a yellow fever certificate is not required to enter Brazil, but Indian health officials require proof of vaccination when you return to India.

Required documents

The official source lists these documents:

  • Online visa application form (signed by both parents for minors)
  • Passport, original and copy: signed, valid for at least 6 months, with at least 2 blank pages
  • Proof of financial capability, such as bank statements for the past three months, an income tax return or a pay slip
  • Passport-style photo that meets ICAO requirements, taken in the last 6 months
  • Flight tickets: a booked itinerary showing entry into and exit from Brazil
  • Personal cover letter with details of the applicant and the trip
  • Hotel reservation or similar document
  • Where relevant, an invitation letter from the company or event organiser, or proof of registration if you are attending an event as a spectator
  • For minors: a visa authorisation form signed by both parents and notarised

Business Visa (VIVIS)Meetings, fairs and business events, signing contracts, audits and consultancyINR 9,600
Business e-visaIndian citizens on business trips of up to 90 days who can e-sign documents through DigiLocker with AadhaarPaid in the e-consular form; the Embassy lists INR 9,600 for visitor visas
TransitLeaving the airport transit zone, or travelling on separate ticketsINR 9,600
Temporary visas (VITEM I, III, IV, V, VII, XI)Study, pre-approved work, family reunion and similar long-stay purposesINR 12,000

These are the regular fees listed by the Embassy of Brazil in New Delhi. The only accepted payment is a bank transfer from the applicant’s own account, so that the applicant’s name shows on the Embassy’s bank statement; cash deposits and payments from an agent’s account are not accepted. Fees are non-refundable, including fees paid for the wrong visa category.

How to apply

  1. Fill in the online visa application form and print the receipt. Completing the form does not submit your application.
  2. Request an appointment through the Embassy’s e-consular system. Do not send documents by email.
  3. Pay the visa fee by bank transfer at the last step of the appointment form.
  4. Wait 24 to 48 hours for the Embassy to validate your request. You then receive an email link to book your appointment.
  5. Go to the Embassy at your appointment time with your passport and all original documents. The submission counter works Monday to Thursday, 10:00 am to 12:00 pm, and walk-in or courier applications are not accepted.
  6. If you live in Maharashtra, Goa or Karnataka, you may apply at the Consulate General of Brazil in Mumbai instead.

Processing time

Up to 10–15 business days; the Embassy cites high demand and advises applying as early as possible. Before that, the Embassy takes 24 to 48 hours to validate your appointment request, and submission slots can be scarce.

Before you apply

  • Visa rules and fees change. Confirm them on the official site listed below before you pay or book.
  • Incomplete applications and missing documents are the most common cause of delay.
  • False information can lead to refusal and affect later applications.

A visa cover letter is a formal document submitted with your visa application that explains the purpose of your visit, travel itinerary, financial means, accommodation details, and your intent to return home. While not always mandatory, a well-written cover letter helps visa officers understand your case quickly and can strengthen your application.

You can track your visa application status online through the official embassy or consulate website using your application reference number. Most visa application centres (such as VFS Global, TLScontact, or BLS International) also provide online tracking portals where you can check your application status in real time.

A single-entry visa allows you to enter the destination country only once during its validity period. A multiple-entry visa permits you to enter and exit the country multiple times. Multiple-entry visas are ideal for frequent travellers and are often valid for longer durations such as 1, 3, or 5 years.

If your visa application is rejected, you will receive a refusal letter explaining the specific reason for the decision. You may be able to reapply by addressing the concerns mentioned, file an appeal if the country's regulations allow it, or request a reconsideration. It is advisable to seek professional advice before reapplying.
