Do Indians need a visa for Burkina Faso?

Indian passport holders need a visa for Burkina Faso. Visas are requested and paid for online through the official e-Visa portal.

Documents required

The official source lists these documents:

  • Valid passport
  • Completed, dated and signed application form
  • Two recent colour photos on a white background, 3.5 × 4.5 cm
  • Proof of the reason for your stay
  • Medical certificate stating you have no contagious disease
  • Health insurance covering your stay
  • Stamp duty payment receipt

Best Time to Visit Burkina Faso

Burkina Faso has a tropical climate with distinct wet and dry seasons. Timing is important for comfortable travel:

  • Dry Season (November to April): The best time to visit with pleasant temperatures of 25-35C. Low humidity and minimal rainfall. Ideal for exploring Ouagadougou, Bobo-Dioulasso, and the Ruins of Loropni.
  • Hot Season (March to May): The hottest period with temperatures reaching 40-45C, particularly in the north. Plan outdoor activities for early morning or late afternoon. The Harmattan wind brings dust from the Sahara.
  • Wet Season (June to October): Heavy rainfall, especially from July to September. Some roads become impassable. However, the landscape is green and the countryside is beautiful. Lower hotel rates.
  • Cultural Events: FESPACO (Panafrican Film and Television Festival of Ouagadougou) takes place in odd-numbered years (February/March) and is Africa's largest film festival. The SIAO International Crafts Fair alternates annually.

Travel Tips for Burkina Faso

Discover the Land of the Upright People with these tips:

  • Currency: West African CFA Franc (XOF) is the local currency. ATMs are available in Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso but can be unreliable. Carry sufficient cash when traveling to smaller towns. Credit cards are accepted only in major hotels.
  • Transportation: Ouagadougou International Airport (OUA) is the main gateway. SOTRACO buses operate within the capital. Intercity travel is by bus or bush taxi. Roads are generally good in urban areas but challenging during the rainy season.
  • Language: French is the official language. Moor, Dioula, and Fulfulde are widely spoken indigenous languages. English is rarely spoken. Learning basic French is essential for travel.
  • Local Customs: Burkina Faso is known for its hospitality — the name means "Land of the Upright People." Greetings are important — always greet people before starting a conversation. Handshakes are the standard greeting. Elders are treated with great respect.
  • Cuisine: Try Riz Gras (fried rice with vegetables and meat), T (millet or corn porridge), Poulet Bicyclette (grilled free-range chicken), and Brochettes (skewered meat). Bissap (hibiscus tea) is a refreshing local drink.
  • Safety: Check current security advisories before travel. Some border regions face security concerns. Register with your embassy on arrival. Avoid non-essential travel to areas near the Mali and Niger borders.
