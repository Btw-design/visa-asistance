Germany Work Visa for Indians: EU Blue Card, Skilled Worker Visa and Opportunity Card

Germany has several work routes, and the right one depends on your qualification and whether you already have a job offer. Indians apply for a national (type D) visa through the German Missions in India, then convert it to a residence permit in Germany.

Overview

A work visa and a residence permit are two stages of the same process. The national visa lets you enter Germany for work; after arrival you receive the residence title, such as the EU Blue Card, from the local foreigners authority.

The EU Blue Card is for people with a degree or a comparable qualification and a job offer above a salary threshold. Skilled worker permits cover recognised university degrees and recognised vocational training. The Opportunity Card is different: it is a job-seeker route and does not need a job offer.

A Schengen visa does not allow you to take up employment in Germany.

Other employment categories, such as deputations and intra-company transfers, are processed differently and take longer.

Eligibility

EU Blue Card

  • A German degree, or a foreign degree comparable to a German one. Tertiary-level qualifications of at least three years can also qualify.
  • A job offer in Germany for at least six months that matches your qualification.
  • A gross annual salary of at least EUR 50,700 (2026).
  • In shortage occupations, such as STEM professionals, doctors, IT managers, nurses and teachers, EUR 45,934.20 (2026) is enough, with Federal Employment Agency approval.
  • New entrants who graduated less than three years ago can also qualify at EUR 45,934.20 (2026).
  • For regulated professions, a licence to practise must be in place or in prospect.

EU Blue Card for IT specialists without a degree

  • A job offer as an IT professional for at least six months.
  • A gross annual salary of at least EUR 45,934.20 (2026).
  • At least three years of IT experience in the past seven, at university level.

Skilled worker visas (Sections 18a and 18b)

  • A German qualification or a recognised foreign one: a university degree for 18b, vocational training for 18a.
  • A concrete job offer for qualified employment.
  • Approval from the Federal Employment Agency for employment.
  • For regulated professions, permission to practise from the recognition authority.
  • If you are 45 or older, a minimum salary or proof of adequate pension provision.

Opportunity Card

  • Recognition as a skilled worker, or at least six points in the points system.
  • Proof that you can support yourself: generally at least EUR 1,091 a month.
  • Applied for online through the Consular Services Portal.

Job offer and sponsorship

Germany has no employer-sponsor licence. You need a concrete job offer or contract for the Blue Card and skilled worker routes, and the employer provides the job details that the Federal Employment Agency assesses.

The exception is the Opportunity Card. It lets you enter without a job offer to look for work, and allows trial work or part-time work of up to 20 hours a week while you search.

Who does what

Work visa applications fail most often when it is unclear who is responsible for which step. This is how the duties are split.

What the employer does

  • Provide a concrete job offer or contract, with salary and job description.
  • Complete the declaration of employment used for Federal Employment Agency approval.
  • Optionally start the fast-track skilled worker procedure in Germany.

What you do

  • Get your foreign qualification recognised or confirmed as comparable.
  • Apply for the visa through the Consular Services Portal and attend your appointment.
  • Show health insurance and, for the Opportunity Card, proof of funds.
  • Register your address and apply for the residence permit after arrival.
  • Notify the foreigners authority if you change jobs in the first year on a Blue Card.

Documents

Common documents

  • Valid passport
  • Completed online application through the Consular Services Portal
  • Biometric photographs
  • Degree or training certificates, with proof of recognition or comparability
  • Proof of health insurance
  • Curriculum vitae

How to apply

1

Check that your qualification is recognised in Germany or comparable to a German one.

2

Secure a job offer, unless you are applying for the Opportunity Card.

3

Apply online through the Federal Foreign Office’s Consular Services Portal and upload the documents on your personalised list.

4

Attend your appointment in India to submit biometrics and originals, and pay the EUR 75 visa fee.

5

Wait for the decision. Blue Card applications and those with Federal Employment Agency approval are decided directly by the German Missions.

6

Travel to Germany, register your address and apply to the local foreigners authority for your residence permit or Blue Card.

Family and job changes

Dependants

EU Blue Card holders can bring their family to Germany under the family reunification rules. The German Missions in India say that the two-week Blue Card processing time also applies to accompanying family members.

Changing employers

With an EU Blue Card you can change employers and start the new job on your valid card. If you change jobs within the first year, you must notify your local foreigners authority, which checks that the new job still meets the Blue Card conditions.

If it does not, you may be given another permit, such as a residence permit for qualified professionals.

Common mistakes

  • Applying before your qualification is recognised or shown to be comparable.
  • A salary in the contract that falls below the threshold for your route.
  • A job that does not match your qualification.
  • Trying to take up employment on a Schengen visa.
  • Treating the Opportunity Card as a full work permit: it allows only trial work and part-time work.
  • Not telling the foreigners authority about a job change in the first Blue Card year.
