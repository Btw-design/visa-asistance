Australia Work Visa for Indians: Skills in Demand Visa and Work and Holiday Visa

The main employer-sponsored route to work in Australia is the Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482), which needs a nomination from an approved sponsor. Indians aged 18 to 30 can also enter a ballot for the Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462).

Overview

Australian work visas are visas with work rights; there is no separate work permit. The Skills in Demand visa lets an employer sponsor a skilled worker for a position it cannot fill with a suitably skilled Australian.

It has three streams. The Core Skills stream is for occupations on the Core Skills Occupation List. The Specialist Skills stream is for higher-paid roles in most professional occupation groups. The Labour Agreement stream is for employers with a labour agreement with the government.

The Work and Holiday visa is a different kind of route: an extended holiday with the right to work to fund it, with no sponsor. A visitor visa does not allow work.

Work routes

RouteWho it is forJob offer or sponsor needed?
Skills in Demand visa, Core Skills streamWorkers nominated in an occupation on the Core Skills Occupation ListYes. Nomination by an approved sponsor
Skills in Demand visa, Specialist Skills streamWorkers in ANZSCO major groups 1, 2, 4, 5 or 6 whose salary meets the Specialist Skills Income ThresholdYes. Nomination by an approved sponsor
Skills in Demand visa, Labour Agreement streamWorkers nominated by an employer with a labour agreementYes. The employer must have a labour agreement in effect
Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462)Indian passport holders aged 18 to 30, selected in a ballotNo sponsor or job offer

The Department of Home Affairs also lists permanent skilled visas, including employer-nominated and points-tested visas. They have separate criteria and are not covered here.

Eligibility

Core Skills stream

  • Nomination to work in an occupation on the Core Skills Occupation List.
  • Pay at the annual market salary rate for the occupation, and no less than the Core Skills Income Threshold.
  • At least 1 year of relevant work experience in the nominated occupation or a related field.
  • A relevant skills assessment, if required for your occupation.
  • Minimum standards of English, unless exempt.
  • Work only for your sponsor or an associated entity, unless exempt.

Specialist Skills stream

  • An occupation in ANZSCO major groups 1, 2, 4, 5 or 6.
  • A nominated salary that meets the Specialist Skills Income Threshold.
  • Nomination by an approved sponsor.
  • The skills and English required for the visa.

Labour Agreement stream

  • Nomination by an employer whose labour agreement with the Australian Government is in effect.
  • The terms set in that agreement.

Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462)

  • Aged 18 to 30 inclusive.
  • A passport from an eligible country; Indian passport holders must first be selected in the pre-application ballot.
  • Applying online from outside Australia.
  • Not accompanied by dependent children.

Job offer and sponsorship

For the Skills in Demand visa, you must be nominated for a skilled position by an approved sponsor. The employer becomes a sponsor, nominates the position and you then apply for the visa.

The Work and Holiday visa is the exception: it needs no sponsor or job offer, but Indian applicants must be randomly selected in the ballot before they can apply.

Who does what

Work visa applications fail most often when it is unclear who is responsible for which step. This is how the duties are split.

What the employer does

  • Be approved as a sponsor.
  • Nominate the skilled position and show it cannot be filled by a suitably skilled Australian.
  • Pay at least the market salary rate and the income threshold for the stream.
  • Hold a labour agreement, for the Labour Agreement stream.

What you do

  • Show the skills and work experience for the occupation.
  • Get a skills assessment if your occupation needs one.
  • Meet the English language requirement.
  • Apply online and pay the visa charge.
  • Work only for your sponsor, unless exempt.

Documents

Common documents

  • Valid passport
  • Details of your sponsor’s nomination
  • Evidence of qualifications and work experience
  • English language test results, unless exempt
  • Health and character documents requested by the Department
  • Skills assessment, if required for your occupation

How to apply

1

Find an employer that is, or will become, an approved sponsor.

2

The employer lodges the nomination for your position.

3

Gather your evidence: experience, qualifications, English and any skills assessment.

4

Apply for the visa online and pay the visa charge.

5

Provide health and character information when asked.

6

Wait for the decision. Do not make travel or work commitments until the visa is granted.

7

For the Work and Holiday visa: register for the ballot, and apply only if you are selected.

Processing time

Stage or routeOfficial timeframe
Skills in Demand visaVaries by stream and sector; use the Department’s visa processing time guide
Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462)An average of around three months is expected

The Department prioritises Skills in Demand applications under a ministerial direction: healthcare, construction, teaching and similar sectors, and the Specialist Skills stream, are processed ahead of others. Published times are a guide only.

Family and job changes

Dependants

Family members can be included in a Skills in Demand application, or can apply later as subsequent entrants to join the main visa holder; the charge for a subsequent entrant also starts from AUD 4,015.

On the Work and Holiday visa you must not be accompanied by dependent children.

Changing employers

Skills in Demand visa holders must work only for their sponsor or an associated entity, unless exempt. Moving to another employer needs a new nomination from an approved sponsor. Check the current visa conditions with the Department before you resign.

Common mistakes

  • An occupation that is not on the Core Skills Occupation List for the stream you chose.
  • A salary below the income threshold or the market salary rate.
  • Less than one year of relevant work experience.
  • No skills assessment where the occupation requires one.
  • Booking travel before the visa is granted.
  • Applying for the Work and Holiday visa without being selected in the ballot.
