Canada Work Permit for Indians: Employer-Specific and Open Work Permits

Canada issues work permits, not work visas. Most Indians need an employer-specific work permit, which requires a job offer and often a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) obtained by the employer. Open work permits exist, but only in specific situations.

Overview

A work permit authorises you to work in Canada. It is separate from the visa that lets you travel there: Indian citizens who are approved for a work permit are also issued the entry document they need.

There are two types. An employer-specific work permit ties you to one employer and needs a job offer. An open work permit lets you work for most employers without a job offer, but Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) grants it only in certain situations.

A visitor visa does not let you work in Canada.

Work routes

RouteWho it is forJob offer or sponsor needed?
Employer-specific work permit, with an LMIAWorkers hired through the Temporary Foreign Worker ProgramYes. The employer obtains the LMIA
Employer-specific work permit, LMIA-exemptWorkers in categories exempt from the LMIAYes. The employer submits an offer of employment and pays a compliance fee
Open work permitPeople in specific situations, such as recent graduates of Canadian institutions and some family members of workers and studentsNo job offer needed

IRCC also runs special processes for groups such as recent graduates, family members of foreign workers and people waiting for permanent residence decisions. Use IRCC’s tool to find the permit you need.

Eligibility

Employer-specific work permit

  • A job offer from a Canadian employer.
  • An LMIA from the employer, unless the job is exempt.
  • The documents your local visa office requires.
  • Biometrics and, where required, a medical exam.

LMIA streams used by employers

  • High-wage and low-wage positions, set by provincial and territorial wage thresholds.
  • The Global Talent Stream.
  • Primary agriculture and caregiver positions.
  • Quebec has its own process.

Open work permit

  • You must be in one of the situations IRCC lists.
  • No job offer or LMIA is needed.
  • An extra open work permit holder fee applies.

Job offer and sponsorship

For an employer-specific permit you need a job offer first. The LMIA is the employer’s application, made to Employment and Social Development Canada, to show the need for a foreign worker. Employers apply through the LMIA Online portal.

Exceptions are LMIA-exempt jobs, where the employer submits an offer of employment through IRCC and pays an employer compliance fee, and open work permits, which need no job offer at all.

Who does what

Work visa applications fail most often when it is unclear who is responsible for which step. This is how the duties are split.

What the employer does

  • Apply for the LMIA, or submit the offer of employment for an LMIA-exempt job.
  • Pay the employer compliance fee of CAD 230 for LMIA-exempt offers.
  • Give you the job offer and the LMIA or offer of employment details for your application.
  • Meet the programme’s compliance obligations.

What you do

  • Apply online to IRCC and pay your work permit fee.
  • Give biometrics.
  • Take a medical exam if required.
  • Carry your documents when you arrive in Canada.
  • After arrival, get a social insurance number.

Documents

Common documents

  • Valid passport
  • Completed online application form
  • Documents required by your local visa office
  • Job offer and employment-related documents
  • Biometrics
  • Medical exam results, where required

How to apply

1

Check which type of work permit you need with IRCC’s tool.

2

For an employer-specific permit, get a job offer. The employer then obtains an LMIA or submits an LMIA-exempt offer of employment.

3

Gather the forms and documents, including those your local visa office requires.

4

Apply online to IRCC from India and pay the fees.

5

Give your biometrics and complete a medical exam if asked.

6

Wait for the decision.

7

Travel to Canada with your documents; the work permit itself is issued when you arrive.

Family and job changes

Dependants

Family members of foreign workers have their own IRCC process, and some can apply for an open work permit. Eligibility rules have changed in recent years, so check IRCC for the current conditions before you plan.

Changing employers

An employer-specific work permit names the employer you may work for. To move to a different employer you need to apply for a new work permit. Confirm the current rules on IRCC’s website before you start a new job.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming a job offer alone is enough: most offers also need an LMIA or an LMIA-exempt offer submitted by the employer.
  • Paying an employer or recruiter for an LMIA or a job offer.
  • Missing the documents required by the local visa office.
  • Delaying biometrics.
  • Working for a different employer than the one named on the permit.
  • Trying to work on a visitor visa.
