Do Indians need a visa for Brunei?

Indian passport holders must obtain a visa from the High Commission of Brunei Darussalam in New Delhi before travelling.

Documents required

The official source lists these documents:

  • Completed visa application form
  • Passport valid for at least 6 months
  • Two recent passport-size photographs on a blue or white background
  • Invitation letter from a sponsor in Brunei and/or a statutory declaration stamped by a magistrate
  • Confirmed travel itinerary with return flight details, and proof of accommodation
  • Bank statement

How to apply

1

Best Time to Visit Brunei

Brunei has an equatorial tropical climate with high humidity and temperatures year-round. The best time depends on rainfall patterns:

  • Dry Season (February to April): The least rainy period with slightly lower humidity. Temperatures range from 24-32C. Good for exploring the water villages (Kampong Ayer) and Ulu Temburong National Park.
  • Wet Season (October to January): Higher rainfall, particularly from November to December. However, rain usually comes in short heavy bursts followed by sunshine. The rainforest is at its most vibrant. Hotel rates are lower.
  • Shoulder Season (May to September): Mixed weather with occasional showers. The Brunei August Festival features cultural performances, food fairs, and sporting events. Sultan's Birthday celebrations in July include parades and fireworks.
  • Year-Round: Brunei's temperature varies little throughout the year (24-33C). The key variable is rainfall. Pack an umbrella regardless of when you visit.

Travel Tips for Brunei

Explore the peaceful Kingdom of Brunei with these tips:

  • Currency: Brunei Dollar (BND) is interchangeable with the Singapore Dollar (SGD) at par. Credit cards are widely accepted in Bandar Seri Begawan's hotels and malls. Cash is essential for markets, taxis, and rural areas.
  • Transportation: Brunei International Airport (BWN) in Bandar Seri Begawan is the main gateway. Cars are the primary mode of transport — renting a car is the most convenient way to explore. Buses are limited and infrequent. Water taxis connect the main city to Kampong Ayer.
  • Language: Malay (Bahasa Melayu) is the official language. English is widely spoken, especially in business and tourism. Mandarin and Chinese dialects are also common.
  • Islamic Culture: Brunei is a conservative Islamic sultanate. Dress modestly — cover shoulders and knees in public. Alcohol is not sold anywhere in the country. Non-Muslims can bring limited alcohol (2 bottles) for personal consumption. During Ramadan, eating and drinking in public during daylight hours is prohibited.
  • Cuisine: Try Ambuyat (sago starch, the national dish), Nasi Katok (rice with fried chicken and sambal), and Satay. Seafood is excellent. The Gadong Night Market offers affordable local food.
  • Attractions: Visit the stunning Jame' Asr Hassanil Bolkiah Mosque, the Royal Regalia Museum, the water village of Kampong Ayer (Venice of the East), and Ulu Temburong National Park (accessible by boat and canopy walkway).
