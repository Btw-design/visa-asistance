Kuwait Work Visa for Indians: Work Permit, Entry Visa and Article 18 Residence

Working in Kuwait’s private sector takes three steps, all led by the employer: a work permit, an entry visa to work, and then an Article 18 residence permit after you arrive.

Overview

The work permit is the labour authority’s approval for a private employer to hire you. Kuwait Government Online lists the entry visa as a Ministry of Interior service that requires the work permit.

After arrival, employees in the private (civil) sector obtain a residence permit under Article 18 of the residence regulations. The work permit, the entry visa and the residence permit are three separate documents.

A visit visa does not authorise employment.

Government-sector employment follows a different residence article. This guide covers private-sector work.

Eligibility

Entry visa to work in the private sector

  • A work permit issued by the labour authority.
  • A sponsor with an authorised signatory.
  • Visa and security application forms signed by the authorised signatory.

Article 18 residence permit, first time

  • The work permit.
  • A health insurance certificate.
  • A disease-free (medical) certificate.
  • A police record certificate.
  • The entry visa and your original passport.

Job offer and sponsorship

A job offer from a Kuwaiti employer is required. The employer is your sponsor and its authorised signatory signs the visa and security forms, together with a sponsor’s declaration and undertaking.

The labour authority checks that the job description matches the activity the company practises, and asks for an educational certificate for roles that need one.

Who does what

Work visa applications fail most often when it is unclear who is responsible for which step. This is how the duties are split.

What the employer does

  • Apply for the work permit.
  • Apply for the entry visa at the immigration department, with the signed forms and the sponsor’s undertaking.
  • Pay the official fees.
  • Apply for your Article 18 residence after you arrive, through the Sahel app or in person.

What you do

  • Provide your passport and an educational certificate where the role needs one.
  • Complete the medical checks for the disease-free certificate.
  • Provide a police record certificate.
  • Hold health insurance for the residence permit.
  • For ECR passports: get emigration clearance in India before departure.

Documents

Common documents

  • Original passport
  • Work permit
  • Two personal photographs
  • Entry visa
  • Health insurance certificate
  • Disease-free (medical) certificate
  • Police record certificate
  • For ECR passport holders: emigration clearance obtained through the eMigrate system before departure

How to apply

1

Accept a job offer from a private employer in Kuwait.

2

The employer applies for your work permit.

3

The employer applies for the entry visa to work, submitting the work permit and signed forms and paying the fee.

4

Complete the medical checks and obtain your police record certificate.

5

If your passport carries an Emigration Check Required (ECR) endorsement, obtain emigration clearance from the Protector of Emigrants through India’s eMigrate system before you fly. This applies to employment in all 18 ECR countries, which include Kuwait.

6

Travel to Kuwait on the entry visa.

7

The employer applies for your first Article 18 residence permit, through the Sahel app or at the residency department.

Family and job changes

Dependants

Kuwait Government Online lists an entry visa for family members to join an employee in the government or private sector. We did not confirm the salary condition for sponsoring family, so check with the Ministry of Interior.

Changing employers

Kuwait Government Online lists a residence transfer service for moving an Article 18 residence, and the Interior Ministry has launched e-services for issuing and transferring it. We did not confirm the conditions, so check with the labour authority before you change jobs.

Common mistakes

  • Working on a visit visa.
  • A job description that does not match the company’s registered activity.
  • Missing the police record certificate or the medical certificate.
  • No health insurance when the residence is applied for.
  • Leaving India without emigration clearance when your passport has an ECR endorsement.
  • Paying agents for a visa without seeing the official fee.
