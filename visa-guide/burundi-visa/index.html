Do Indians need a visa for Burundi?

Indian passport holders need a visa for Burundi. Get it before you travel: official Burundian sources do not agree on whether visas are currently issued on arrival.

Burundi visa options for Indians

OptionWho it is forStay and validityOfficial fee
Entry visa, one month, single entryTourism, business and visits1 month; renewable at most twiceUS$90

Burundi visa fees

OptionOfficial fee
Entry visa, one month, single entryUS$90
Transit visaUS$40

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs describes the US$90 entry visa as available online, at Bujumbura airport and at embassies. Burundi’s embassy in Ottawa, however, states that it is currently impossible to get a visa at the airport. Because of that conflict, do not rely on a visa on arrival.

Documents required

Official sources do not give a full list for every case; these are the documents applicants are normally asked for. Confirm the exact list when you apply.

  • Passport valid for at least 6 months, with blank pages
  • Completed visa application form
  • Recent passport-size photographs
  • Confirmed return or onward ticket
  • Hotel booking or address of stay
  • Recent bank statements showing funds for the trip
  • International certificate of vaccination (yellow fever)

How to apply

1

Apply before travel, either online through the General Commission of Migrations (migration.gov.bi) or at a Burundian embassy.

2

Pay the visa fee in US dollars.

3

Carry the visa approval with your passport.

4

To stay longer than a month, apply for an extension at the migration office in Burundi.

Processing time

Burundi’s embassy in Ottawa quotes about 15 days for applications made through an embassy.

Best Time to Visit Burundi

Burundi has a tropical highland climate moderated by altitude. The country offers pleasant temperatures year-round:

  • Dry Season (June to September): The best time to visit with sunny days and minimal rainfall. Temperatures of 20-28C in Bujumbura and cooler in the highlands. Ideal for wildlife viewing in Rusizi National Park and trekking in the Kibira National Park.
  • Short Dry Season (December to January): A brief dry spell between the two rainy seasons. Good conditions for travel with lower rainfall than the wet seasons.
  • Wet Seasons (February to May, October to November): Heavy rainfall, particularly from March to May. Roads can be challenging. The landscape is lush and Lake Tanganyika is beautiful. Fewer tourists and lower hotel rates.
  • Lake Tanganyika: The lake's beaches are enjoyable year-round with water temperatures of 24-29C. The best months for water activities are June-September and December-January.

Travel Tips for Burundi

Explore the heart of Africa with these practical tips:

  • Currency: Burundian Franc (BIF) is the local currency. ATMs are available in Bujumbura but limited elsewhere. US Dollars are accepted at hotels and some restaurants. Credit card acceptance is very limited outside major hotels.
  • Transportation: Bujumbura Melchior Ndadaye International Airport (BJM) is the main gateway. Taxis are available in Bujumbura — negotiate the fare before starting. Buses and shared taxis connect Bujumbura to other towns. The road network is limited.
  • Language: Kirundi and French are the official languages. Swahili is also widely spoken, particularly in Bujumbura and along Lake Tanganyika. English is rarely spoken outside international organizations.
  • Local Customs: Burundian society values politeness and respect. Greetings are important — always take time to greet people properly. Use both hands when giving or receiving items. Dress modestly, especially in rural areas.
  • Cuisine: Try Ugali (maize porridge, staple food), Beans with Plantains, Grilled Fish from Lake Tanganyika (delicious), and Brochettes. Bananas feature in many dishes — including banana beer (Urwagwa), a traditional fermented drink.
  • Attractions: Visit the Source of the Nile (the southernmost source of the Nile River), Rusizi National Park (hippos and birds), the Kibira National Park (rainforest with chimpanzees), and the stunning Lake Tanganyika beaches near Bujumbura. The Gitega National Museum showcases Burundian royal history.
